Phishing Attacks

Phishing is a social engineering attack in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted organisation, such as a bank, a delivery company or an internal IT department, in order to trick a person into revealing sensitive information or running malicious software. Most phishing campaigns arrive by email, but the same technique is used over SMS (smishing), voice calls (vishing) and messaging apps.

A typical phishing email creates a sense of urgency. It may claim that an account will be suspended, that a payment has failed, or that a parcel is waiting for delivery. The message contains a link to a fake login page that looks almost identical to the real website. When the victim enters a username and password, the credentials are sent directly to the attacker.

Spear phishing is a targeted form of phishing aimed at a specific person or department. The attacker researches the victim on social media or the company website and writes a highly personalised message. Whaling is spear phishing aimed at senior executives, for example a fake request from the CEO asking the finance team to make an urgent wire transfer. This last scenario is also known as Business Email Compromise (BEC).

Warning signs of phishing include a sender address that does not match the organisation's real domain, spelling mistakes, generic greetings such as "Dear customer", unexpected attachments, and links whose real destination (visible when hovering over them) differs from the text shown.

Defences against phishing include security awareness training, email filtering with SPF, DKIM and DMARC checks, multi-factor authentication (MFA) so that a stolen password alone is not enough, and a simple process that lets employees report suspicious emails to the security team. Phishing remains the most common initial access method used in real-world breaches.

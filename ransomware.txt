Ransomware

Ransomware is a type of malware that encrypts the victim's files or locks the entire system and then demands a payment, usually in cryptocurrency, in exchange for the decryption key. It is one of the most damaging threats for hospitals, schools, government departments and businesses because it can halt operations completely.

Modern ransomware groups often use double extortion. Before encrypting the data, they copy sensitive files out of the network. If the victim refuses to pay, the attackers threaten to publish the stolen data on a leak website. Some groups go further with triple extortion, adding DDoS attacks or contacting the victim's customers directly.

Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) is a business model in which developers rent their ransomware to affiliates, who carry out the attacks and share the profit. This has lowered the skill level needed to launch an attack.

Common entry points are phishing emails, exposed Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) services with weak passwords, and unpatched VPN or firewall devices. After gaining access, attackers move laterally, try to obtain administrator privileges, and delete or encrypt backups before launching the encryption.

The most important defence is a reliable backup strategy. The 3-2-1 rule recommends keeping three copies of data, on two different types of storage, with one copy offline or immutable so that ransomware cannot reach it. Backups should be tested regularly by actually restoring data. Other defences include MFA on remote access, network segmentation to limit lateral movement, EDR on all endpoints, and an incident response plan that has been practised in advance.

Security agencies generally advise victims not to pay the ransom, because payment does not guarantee recovery of the data and it funds further criminal activity.
